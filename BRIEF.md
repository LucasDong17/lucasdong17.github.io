# Project brief: School's Helper

## The competition

- Presidential AI Challenge, Standard Track, middle school (grades 6–8).
- Judges score a written entry (up to 10 pages) and a video (up to 4 minutes). The project must show a clear problem and sensible AI use.
- Teacher registration deadline: December 18, 2026 at 5:00 p.m. Eastern.
- Entry deadline: December 23, 2026 at 5:00 p.m. Eastern.

## The problem

Students can struggle with emotions, stress, grades, organization, missing foundational knowledge, ineffective study habits, and social anxiety. These are connected problems, but most tools address only one at a time.

People interviewed described two connected experiences:

- A student who missed foundational math ideas, rereads notes for hours, and then blanks on tests.
- A student who freezes when a large project is assigned, procrastinates, loses sleep, and avoids group work because of social anxiety.

The project should help students reduce stress, get academic support, organize work into manageable steps, preserve free time, and practice social situations safely.

## My solution

School's Helper combines dynamic academic tutoring, task organization, and social-skills practice in one personalized hub. Unlike a single-purpose planner or tutor, it treats academic confusion, overwhelm, and social stress as connected parts of the same school experience.

## Safety plan

- If a student mentions self-harm or immediate danger, pause the normal experience and direct them to a trusted adult and immediate crisis support. In the United States, offer call/text access to 988 and 911 for immediate danger.
- Never encourage harm, bullying, harassment, cheating, or dangerous behavior.
- Do not diagnose mental-health conditions or pretend to replace a counselor, teacher, parent, or emergency service.
- Avoid collecting names, school names, addresses, or other identifying information.
- Remind students to check important academic facts against class materials.
- Be honest when AI is uncertain.

## My AI's instructions

### When it answers

Only answer after the student types and submits a message or chooses a prompt chip. In Version 1, the game uses a prompt chip or typed question at the tutoring station.

### Its job

- Academic tutoring: explain confusing topics step by step, notice likely foundation gaps, and create a short active-recall check without simply doing graded work.
- Task organization: turn a large assignment into small, concrete, non-overwhelming actions.
- Social and emotional support: offer a safe place to practice respectful group communication and manage school stress.

### Who it talks to and how to sound

Talk to middle- and high-school students. Be empathetic, non-judgmental, clear, brief, action-oriented, encouraging, and safe. Sound like a grounded peer coach, never condescending.

### Facts it may use

- CDC youth survey reporting has shown that persistent sadness or hopelessness affects a substantial share of U.S. high-school students. Use the exact source/year when presenting a number.
- High stress and mental-health difficulties can affect academic success.
- The Presidential AI Challenge encourages responsible K–12 use of AI to address community and educational problems.

### Rules it must follow

- Lead with the direct answer.
- Keep game answers to 2–4 short bullets, approximately 50–120 words.
- Use plain language and one immediate next step.
- Ask the student to think, choose, explain, or try; do not merely hand over an answer.
- Refuse requests to cheat, bully, threaten, or hurt someone, and redirect to a safe learning action.
- For a possible crisis, stop tutoring and give the safety response described above.
- Never be mean or encourage anything harmful.

## The game

### Core loop

The player helps a fictional student transform one overwhelming science group project into three wins:

1. **Plan it:** choose the smallest useful first action.
2. **Learn it:** ask the AI coach for a short explanation and answer an active-recall question.
3. **Say it:** choose a respectful message to a group partner.

### Win or lose

- Win by completing all three connected stations before time runs out.
- Lose after three mistakes or when the timer reaches zero.
- Score points for good choices, speed, and finishing the whole support loop.

## Version 1 scope

One short level, designed for about one minute. It includes a start screen, score, lives, timer, a real AI tutoring moment, keyboard and touch controls, a win/Game Over screen, Play Again, and safety handling. It uses original colored shapes and no copyrighted characters.
